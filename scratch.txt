It started with a goal to shave nine hours off a 3D print: taking a custom wedge base from a 17-hour crawl down to an 8-hour sprint using adaptive cubic infill. Once that rock-solid foundation was off the plate and curing on the desk, it was time to move on to the isolation layer—a 1mm Filaflex mat designed to sit flush in the wedge's recessed shelf. To get that factory-gasket look, the edges were left completely square, skipping the brim entirely to avoid flexible filament tear-out, and prepped with a thin, wiped-down layer of hairspray on the print bed for a clean release.With the 3D-printed isolated bases squared away, attention turned to the heart of the setup: a Jolida JD202A tube integrated amplifier. The amp had been sitting wait for a proper refresh, carrying a bit of history with it—having been picked up years ago from Andy Singer’s legendary Sound by Singer shop on 16th Street, back during the late 90s era of grab-and-go lunches near Chat 'n' Chew and Union Square.The interconnects were sorted first. Comparing a modern, shiny blue WireWorld Luna (DNA Helix flat-wire geometry) against a classic, matte-green AudioQuest Emerald (Hyperlitz solid-core geometry), the vintage Emeralds took the spot for the main analog link. Their heavy directional shielding—grounded only at the source end so RF noise drains backward away from the amp—was built for keeping low-level signal paths dead quiet.Inside the chassis, the re-wiring and component audit began in earnest:Wire Selection: Cheap Amazon 18 AWG PVC wire was skipped in favor of 18 AWG stranded tinned-copper wire with PTFE (Teflon) insulation. The PTFE jacket offers high dielectric strength with a ultra-thin outer diameter, tight twisting for AC heater lines to eliminate 60Hz hum, a 600V rating for B+ high-voltage rails, and absolute immunity to soldering iron melt-back.Labeling for the Future: To ensure future work is straightforward, high-temperature wire labeling was planned using Brother HSe heat-shrink cartridges (5.8mm / 0.23" tubing shrunk over the 18 AWG PTFE) to create permanent, heat-resistant identifiers near key nodes.Tube Socket Mapping: The schematic was cross-referenced with the chassis silkscreen to confirm the front-row small-signal layout ($V2, V1, V5, V6$). The two high-gain 12AX7 preamp tubes occupy the center sockets ($V1$ and $V5$), flanked by the 12AT7 driver/phase inverter tubes on the far edges ($V2$ and $V6$), driving the quad of EL34 power tubes ($V3, V4, V8, V7$) in the rear row.Resistor Sorting: Color codes and meter readings were verified across the board:A 5-band precision resistor reading Blue-Gray-Black-Red-Brown right-to-left confirmed $68\text{k}\Omega$ at 1% tolerance.For the main power board, flameproof pink metal oxide film (MOF) 5% resistors were chosen over blue metal film to handle high-voltage supply drops, heat, and potential fault conditions safely.With the chassis laid out cleanly, high-temp components locked into place, and custom isolation wedges ready underneath, the classic Jolida is rebuilt and ready to drive audio for decades to come.
